* Branch
A branch represents a coherent state of one or more multigroups at a
particular point in their history. It holds the references to the
multigroups visible from that state and provides an isolated context
in which subsequent changes may be expressed.

Relations are sets and are therefore not mutated in the set-theoretic
sense. Change in the represented world is instead understood as a
succession of relation values. The relation predicate establishes the
membership criteria of a relation, while its extension consists of the
tuples currently asserted to satisfy those criteria. Ordinary changes
in facts therefore alter the extension without necessarily altering
the intension.

Consider an EMPLOYEES relation representing the employees of a
company. Hiring a new employee does not ordinarily change what it
means to be an employee. Rather, a tuple representing the newly hired
employee becomes an asserted member of EMPLOYEES in a later database
state. The predicate and constraints defining admissible membership
may remain invariant while the extension differs between the earlier
and later states.

A branch identifies one such coherent state and provides the basis
from which another may be produced. It therefore gives RNT a
representation of change without requiring mutation to be part of the
relational semantics. How successive states are physically
represented, shared, or persisted is an implementation concern.

* Schematics
Schematics describes the logical constitution of objects known to
RNT. For a relation, it describes its attributes, the domains on which
those attributes are defined, its predicate and constraints, and its
dependencies on other relations.

An attribute represents a property in the context of a particular
group. Its domain establishes the kind of values the attribute may
assume, while constraints establish the conditions under which tuples
constitute admissible members of the relation. Constraints involving
multiple relations place an individual relation within the wider
semantics of its multigroup.

Schematics therefore describes more than the shape of a relation. It
records the information necessary to understand the logical conditions
under which a relation value is a semantically consistent
representation of its intended group.

* Namespace
A namespace is a component of RNT's hierarchical object/name space. It
provides a scope in which names are associated with runtime objects
and through which those objects may be located.

Namespaces form a tree, allowing objects to be organized under paths
such as =/system=. This hierarchy is an administrative property of the
kernel rather than a relational property of the objects contained
within it. The fact that one object appears beneath another namespace
does not, by itself, assert a relationship between the things
represented by those objects.

Namespaces therefore provide naming, organization, lookup, and scoping
without imposing additional semantics on relations or multigroups.

* Transaction
A transaction is a bounded context in which tentative changes are
performed against a branch. It begins from a particular database state
and accumulates changes without making those changes part of the
committed state until the transaction successfully commits.

A transaction provides a protocol through which operations may be
committed or rolled back. Committing produces an accepted successor
state from the accumulated changes, subject to the fitting
constraints. Rolling back discards those changes and leaves the
originating state unaffected.

Transactions participate in the kernel lifecycle. Disposing of a
transaction that still contains uncommitted changes causes those
changes to be rolled back. Thus tentative state cannot accidentally
become durable merely because the runtime object representing a
transaction ceases to be referenced.

* Domain
A domain is the logical representation of a property and establishes
the values and operations appropriate to that property. Attributes
appearing in relations are defined on domains.

A domain is not merely a physical data type. Its purpose is to give
values their logical interpretation and to establish the operations
and constraints suited to them. Different attributes may be defined on
the same domain when they represent the same kind of property in
different group contexts.

Domains therefore form part of the semantic foundation on which
relation predicates and constraints are expressed.

* Group
A group is represented in RNT by a relation. The relation has an
intension, expressed through its predicate and constraints, and an
extension consisting of the tuples asserted as its members in a
particular database state.

RNT distinguishes relations according to how their membership is
established and the role they play in computation.

** Substantial Relation
A substantial relation belongs to the factual basis of a
multigroup. Its asserted tuples represent facts supplied directly
about the world being represented rather than facts obtained solely by
relational derivation.

For example, EMPLOYEE may be substantial because assertions and
retractions of employee tuples represent substantive changes in the
state of the company. Hiring an employee adds a fact to the
represented state; an employee leaving causes the corresponding fact
no longer to be asserted in a subsequent state.

"Substantial" does not mean physically stored. Whether a substantial
relation is materialized, reconstructed, cached, or represented by
some other storage technique is a physical implementation matter. What
makes it substantial is its logical role as part of the factual basis
from which other facts may be derived.

** Ephemeral Relation
An ephemeral relation is produced through relational operations over
other relations. Selection, projection, join, and the other relational
operations yield relations in their own right, and these results may
exist as runtime objects without becoming part of the substantial
factual basis of a multigroup.

For example, selecting employees whose salary is greater than 1000 and
projecting particular attributes produces an ephemeral relation
derived from EMPLOYEE. Its tuples are consequences of the facts
asserted in EMPLOYEE together with the conditions introduced by the
relational expression, which are part of its intension.

Ephemeral relations retain provenance and lineage. Provenance
identifies the origins of their attributes and other semantic
components, while lineage records the derivation through which the
relation was produced. This permits RNT to preserve and infer
applicable constraints rather than treating an algebraic result as an
anonymous collection of tuples.

Ephemerality concerns the runtime existence of such a derived value. A
derived relation may subsequently be named, cached, persisted, or
otherwise given a longer lifecycle without changing the relational
fact that it was obtained by derivation.

** Procedural Relation
A procedural relation exposes a procedure through a relational
interface. Rather than obtaining its extension from a set of asserted
base facts or solely from relational algebra over existing relations,
membership is established through computation.

For example, PLUS[A, B, C] may represent the relation for which C is
the sum of A and B, while GREATER_THAN_NATURAL[X, Y] may represent
ordered pairs of natural numbers for which X is greater than Y. Such
relations can participate in relational expressions in the same manner
as other relational operands where their evaluation properties permit
it.

A procedural relation need not have a finitely enumerable
extension. Some may be countably infinite, making it impossible to
materialize their complete extension as an ordinary runtime relation
value. RNT must therefore distinguish the ability to decide
membership, enumerate members, establish the cardinality of a
particular invocation, and guarantee termination of an expression
consuming the relation.

Sol, as RNT's total relational language, admits procedural relations
only where the required evaluation properties can be established. An
unrestricted procedure must not silently introduce partiality or
nontermination into an otherwise total relational expression.

* Multigroup
A multigroup represents a collection of related groups and is
represented logically by a database of relations considered together
under common constraints.

Relations within a multigroup are not independent merely because each
has its own predicate. Constraints may involve multiple relations and
thereby express conditions that must hold of the database as a
whole. The multigroup provides the context in which these
interrelation constraints acquire their meaning.

A multigroup is consequently the principal unit of coherent relational
state exposed through a branch. A branch may reference multiple
multigroups and determine the particular state of each that is visible
from that view of the world.

* Session
A session contains state specific to an interaction between a client
environment and the RNT kernel. It establishes the execution context
within which requests are interpreted and kernel objects are accessed.

Session state may include such things as the active identity,
namespace, branch, transaction, open handles, capabilities, and
frontend-specific context. This information governs how a client
interacts with the kernel but does not alter the logical meaning of
the relations being accessed.

A session therefore provides continuity across operations while
maintaining the separation between frontend concerns and relational
semantics. Different frontends may establish different kinds of
sessions while ultimately operating on the same relational kernel.

* Clock
A clock is a kernel coordination object responsible for ordering and
dispatching events. It provides infrastructure for work that must
occur in response to time, state transitions, or other observable
kernel events.

A clock may schedule periodic or deferred services, coordinate event
delivery, and provide interception points for workflows. It may also
participate in mechanisms commonly associated with triggers, where an
event causes additional work to be dispatched.

Such procedural reactions must remain distinct from relational
constraints. A constraint determines whether a database state is
semantically admissible; an event handler determines what computation
should occur in response to an event. A constraint must therefore not
depend for its truth on whether a scheduled or triggered procedure
happened to execute.

Clock time is likewise distinct from relational time. The clock
coordinates execution in the running system, whereas branches and
successive database states represent change in the information managed
by RNT. A correspondence between the two exists only when that
correspondence is explicitly established.
